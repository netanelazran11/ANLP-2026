The failure type investigated is \textbf{implicit negative sentiment}: sentences
that are negative in meaning but contain no explicit negative vocabulary.
Negativity must be inferred from understatement or real-world consequence
(e.g., \emph{``The battery lasts just long enough to get from my bedroom to the kitchen.''}).
The hypothesis is that models rely on surface lexical cues; when those cues are
absent they default to \texttt{Positive}.

\section*{Hypothesis Tests}
% ============================================================

Eight controlled inputs varying only the explicitness of negativity on one
scenario (an umbrella). H1--H3 use explicit negative vocabulary (model should
be correct); H4--H6 use pure understatement with no negative words (predicted
failure); H7--H8 are positive controls.

\begin{table}[h!]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{c L{7cm} c c c}
\toprule
\# & Input & Gold & Qwen3-8B & Llama-3.1-8B \\
\midrule
H1 & This umbrella broke in the first minute of rain and I got completely soaked.
   & Neg & Neg & Neg \\
H2 & This umbrella is useless. It fell apart immediately and left me drenched.
   & Neg & Neg & Neg \\
H3 & Oh great, another umbrella that lasts all of ten seconds in the rain.
   & Neg & Neg & Neg \\
H4 & This umbrella kept me dry for the first ten seconds of the storm.
   & Neg & \textbf{Pos} & Neg \\
H5 & The battery lasts just long enough to reach the coffee machine from my desk.
   & Neg & Neg & Neg \\
H6 & I used this umbrella once before going back to my old one.
   & Neg & Neg & \textbf{Neu} \\
H7 & This umbrella is fantastic. It kept me completely dry through two hours of heavy rain.
   & Pos & Pos & Pos \\
H8 & This umbrella handled a two-hour downpour without a single leak.
   & Pos & Pos & Pos \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

Both models correctly classify explicit negatives (H1--H3) and positive controls
(H7--H8). Qwen3-8B fails on H4 as predicted; Llama produces \texttt{Neutral} on
H6. The pattern confirms the hypothesis: \emph{the more implicit the negativity,
the more likely the model is to fail}.

% ============================================================
\section*{Proposed Solution}
% ============================================================

\textbf{Two-step prompting}: first ask the model to paraphrase what the sentence
implies about the product (\emph{``In one sentence, describe what the following
review implies about the product's quality: [input]''}), then classify that
generated text. The paraphrase will contain explicit evaluative language, making
the classification straightforward. This requires no extra training, only one
additional forward pass.
